# The five calls

**Idea 8's validation test. No code. One afternoon. It can kill the idea for the
price of five phone calls.**

---

## What you are actually testing

Not *"is this useful?"* — every contractor will say yes to a free useful thing.

**You are testing whether they will stand in a kitchen and ask a homeowner for
$250.**

That is a completely different question, and it is the only one that matters,
because the entire business model rests on the contractor selling the record on.
If they will use it but not sell it, the $49/month product still fails — you just
find out eighteen weeks later instead of this afternoon.

**Everything below is designed to stop you from getting a polite yes.**

## Before you dial

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Residential remodelers doing whole-room work — kitchens, baths, additions. 2–15 people. Walls come open on their jobs |
| **Not** | Handymen, painters, roofers, GCs who only sub out. No open walls, no product |
| **How many** | Five who fit. Expect 12–15 dials to get five real conversations |
| **When** | 7:00–8:00am or 4:30–6:00pm. Mid-morning you are interrupting a job |
| **How long** | Six minutes. Say so at the start and mean it |
| **Record it?** | Only with permission. Otherwise take notes immediately after — you will forget the exact words, and the exact words are the data |

**Do not email.** An email gets you a considered, polite, useless answer. You need
the pause before they speak.

## The script

### 1 — Opener (15 seconds)

> "Hi — is this [name]? My name's Sam, I build software for remodelers. I'm not
> selling anything, I'm six minutes into figuring out whether something is worth
> building. Can I ask you three questions about how you close up walls?"

**If they hesitate:** *"Genuinely nothing to buy. There's no product yet — that's
the point of the call."*

**Why it works.** "I'm not selling" is true, and it is the only thing that gets a
contractor to keep talking at 7am.

### 2 — The setup question (their world, not yours)

> **"When you open up a wall on a remodel and there's stuff in there nobody
> expected — what does that cost you, typically?"**

**Shut up and let them talk.** This question exists to do three things: get them
talking about their own money, establish that the problem is real *in their
words*, and give you the language to use later.

**Listen for:** a dollar figure, a specific job, the word "change order."

### 3 — The second setup question

> **"And when somebody needs to cut into a wall you closed up two or three years
> ago — how do they know what's back there?"**

**Listen for:** "they don't", "we guess", "cut an exploratory hole", "I might
still have photos on my phone somewhere."

**That last one is gold.** If they mention photos on their phone, ask:

> *"Could you find them right now if I asked?"*

The answer is almost always no. **That is the whole problem, said by them.**

### 4 — THE QUESTION

Ask it exactly like this. Do not soften it, do not add features, do not say "AI"
or "LiDAR" or "3D."

> **"Say there was an app. Twenty minutes, walls already open, and it produced a
> permanent file for the homeowner showing exactly where every pipe, wire and duct
> is — measured, so they could find it years later. It opens in any browser,
> forever, no login.**
>
> **Would you put $250 on the invoice for it?"**

### 5 — Then say nothing

**Count to five in your head. Do not fill the silence.** The pause is the
measurement. A fast yes and a slow yes are different data.

### 6 — The follow-up that decides it

Whatever they said:

> **"What would you actually charge for it?"**

Their own number is worth more than agreement with yours. Then:

> **"And who would you say it's for — you, or the homeowner?"**

If they say *"it's for me, I'd use it on my own jobs"* — **that is a soft no.**
It means an internal tool, which is a $49 cost, not a $250 revenue line. Mark it
as such however enthusiastic they sounded.

### 7 — Close

> "That's really helpful. Last thing — if I build it, can I come do one on your
> next job for free so you can see it?"

**A yes here is worth more than the answer to the price question**, because it is
a small real commitment rather than an opinion. Get a date if you can.

## Scoring — decide this before you dial

Score each call **before** making the next one, while it is fresh.

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **2 — Strong yes** | Said yes without a long pause, named their own price at $150 or above, said it is for the homeowner, agreed to a free first one |
| **1 — Soft yes** | Yes, but hesitated; or named a price under $150; or said "depends on the client"; or said they would use it themselves |
| **0 — No** | Wouldn't charge for it; couldn't see the homeowner caring; wouldn't want the conversation at closing |

### The decision rule

| Total across 5 calls | Verdict |
|---:|---|
| **8–10** | **Build it.** Start the 90-day plan |
| **5–7** | **Real but mispriced.** Re-run five more calls at their median number before building |
| **0–4** | **Stop.** The latent-value problem is real. No product fixes a thing people will not sell |

**Write the rule down before you dial and hold to it.** The failure mode here is
not a wrong answer — it is talking yourself into an 6 because you already like the
idea.

## The four ways you will fool yourself

1. **Leading the witness.** *"Wouldn't it be great if…"* gets agreement, not data.
   Ask a flat question and let the silence be uncomfortable.
2. **Selling instead of asking.** The moment you start explaining how good it is,
   the call is over as a test. If you catch yourself pitching, stop and ask a
   question.
3. **Counting enthusiasm as a yes.** *"Man, that's a great idea"* is a zero until
   they name a price and say the homeowner would pay it.
4. **Talking to the wrong people.** Five friendly contractors who do not open
   walls tells you nothing. Qualify on the open-wall question first.

---

## What to record beyond the score

Two sentences per call, in their words:

- **The most expensive open-wall surprise they described**, with the dollar figure.
  This becomes the headline on the landing page.
- **Exactly how they described finding old photos.** This becomes the demo's
  opening line.

**Even if all five say no, these two things are worth the afternoon** — they are
the raw material for whichever idea you build instead.

## After the calls

**8–10:** go to `08-behind-the-wall.md` §13 and start week 1. Also run the
triangulation weekend prototype — the two tests are independent and both gate the
build.

**5–7:** five more calls at their median price. If it holds, build at that price
and update the Dials tab — at $99/mo instead of $49, break-even halves from 145
customers to 72.

**0–4:** stop, and go run idea 2's Test 1 instead. It is one week, it settles the
hardest question in the highest-LTV business of the five, and the market sizing
says it needs only 0.40% penetration.
