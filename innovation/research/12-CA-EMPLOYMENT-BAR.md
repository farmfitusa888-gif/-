# How big is the California plaintiff-side employment bar?

Research note filling the `[NOT ESTABLISHED]` in `buildouts/01-backpay/PLAN.md` §4,
Segment A. Written 2026-09-01. Network conditions: every relevant host was blocked
by the egress proxy (cela.org, labor.ca.gov, calbar.ca.gov, supreme.courts.ca.gov,
cabia.org, propublica.org, justia, avvo, martindale, superlawyers, cornell LII).
Search summaries were the instrument. Nothing below is labelled `[verified]`,
because I did not open a single primary document with my own eyes. Read the
labels; they are load-bearing.

---

## VERDICT

| | |
|---|---|
| **Firms in California actively doing wage-and-hour class or PAGA work** | **~400** |
| **Defensible range** | **350 – 600** |
| **Confidence** | **Moderate.** Two independent methods converge on ~400. Neither was read at source. |
| **100 paying firms as a share of that** | **17% at the high end of the range, 29% at the low end. Best point estimate 25%.** |
| **Verdict on the 100-firm target** | Not 5%. Not 80%. **A quarter of the market.** Achievable in kind, aggressive in timeframe, and the wrong shape for one price. |
| **Concentration** | **Steep.** 5 firms take ~24% of filings, 20 firms take 50%. The other ~376 split the rest, roughly a dozen notices each per year. |

The single most useful number in this document: **396 firms filed at least one
PAGA case in California in the twelve months ending May 2026.** That is a count
of behaviour rather than self-description, which is why it beats every directory
figure below by a wide margin.

The plan's §4 asks whether 101 firms is 5% or 80% of the market. Answer: neither,
and the question was the wrong one. 25% is the number, and the harder problem
that replaces it is that a quarter of *this* market is not one product.

## 1. CELA

The plan named CELA as the thing that would settle it. It half does.

| Claim | Figure | Label |
|---|---|---|
| CELA membership, current, per cela.org | **over 1,400 California attorneys** | `[review]` |
| CELA membership, per a public comment letter CELA filed with the LWDA on 23 March 2026 in the PAGA rulemaking | **"a statewide non-profit organization of over 1,400 attorneys"** | `[review]`, primary-quality source, could not open the PDF |
| CELA membership, GuideStar profile | **approximately 1,340** | `[review]` |
| Older cela.org pages still live | "more than 1,300"; "over 1,200" | `[conflict]` |
| CELA member *firm* count | — | `[NOT ESTABLISHED]`. CELA does not publish it. |
| CELA revenue, FY2024 (Form 990) | $1.7M revenue, $1.17M expenses, $1.64M assets | `[review]` |

The 1,200 / 1,300 / 1,400 spread is almost certainly page-staleness rather than
disagreement. Different corners of cela.org were last edited in different years.
The March 2026 rulemaking comment is the most recent and the most formal, and it
says 1,400.

The 990 does not independently confirm the headcount. At plausible dues of
$300–600 a year, 1,400 members generate $420k–840k, and the conference makes up
the rest of $1.7M. Consistent, not probative. I looked for the dues line and
could not reach the filing.

**Membership qualification matters more than the count.** CELA describes members
as attorneys "who devote the major portion of their practices to representing
employees." That is a self-certified filter, and it is the filter I want: it
excludes defence-side lawyers by construction. Every directory in §3 fails to do
this and is worth less as a result.

**Converting 1,400 attorneys to firms.** Plaintiff-side employment practices in
California are small. Boutiques, two-to-six-lawyer shops, and a meaningful number
of solos. At 3.5 attorneys per firm, 1,400 attorneys is **400 firms**. At 3, it
is 467. At 4, it is 350. That divisor is my judgement, not a source, and it is
the weakest link in this section.

Two things are worth pulling out.

**Super Lawyers is the only directory that splits the sides.** In the Los Angeles
metro its plaintiff:defence ratio is 439:294, or **60:40**. If that ratio holds
statewide, Martindale's 29,090 implies ~17,000 plaintiff-side employment lawyers
in California. That number is obviously wrong by an order of magnitude against
CELA's 1,400, and the reason is that Martindale's category is self-tagged and
free. It tells you how many lawyers are willing to say they do employment law,
not how many do.

**The gap between 29,090 and 1,400 is the whole problem with directory counts.**
Twenty-to-one. I would not use any figure in this section for anything except
proving that directories cannot size this market.

### The number that sizes the market

> **In the twelve months ending May 2026, 396 firms filed at least one PAGA case
> in California. 20 firms accounted for 50% of filings. Five firms accounted for
> about a quarter.** `[review]`

Source: Ankura Consulting Group, "The 2025 to 2026 PAGA Trend Update," July 2026,
restated independently by the California Employment Law Report in July 2026. Two
appearances, one origin, so treat it as one source seen twice.

I trust this more than anything else in the document. It counts firms by what
they did, in a fixed window, from filing records rather than marketing pages.

### Concentration, from the agency's own data

From the LWDA Initial Statement of Reasons, FY 2024–25 `[review]` throughout:

- Total notices: **8,846**
- Five law firms filed **2,086** of them, **23.6%**
- Ten attorneys filed **2,192**
- The busiest single attorney filed **597**; the busiest firm **605**; second **535**
- Three firms averaged more than one notice per day for the year
- **Eight firms and four attorneys** would have crossed the proposed
  "high-frequency filer" line of 200 notices in twelve months, and together they
  account for **more than a third of all notices**

Cumulative filing counts since inception, from CABIA's tracker `[review]`:
Moon & Yang ~1,763 since 2010; Wilshire Law Firm ~1,744 since 2016; Lawyers for
Justice ~1,578; Bibiyan Law Group ~1,370; Aegis ~1,339; Lavi & Ebrahimian ~1,309;
Younessi ~1,266; Blumenthal Nordrehaug ~1,232; James Hawkins ~1,144; Capstone
~1,051.

### The settlement side

First half of 2026: roughly **1,400–1,500 settlements**, about **$1.3 billion**
paid, averaging ~$219M/month. The five most active plaintiff firms took about
**40% of settlements and slightly over 40% of settlement dollars**. About half of
settlements came in **under $500,000**, with $100k–500k the most common band.
44% covered fewer than 200 employees. `[review]`

For 2025: ~2,400 PAGA suits settled for **$2.2 billion**, of which **$740 million**
went to attorneys. `[review]`, and note that this one comes from CABIA via GV Wire,
which is advocacy.

**A caution on every figure in this section.** All of it reaches me through
employer-side sources: defence firms, a defence consultancy, and a business
advocacy group. Their interest runs toward making the plaintiff bar look small,
concentrated and rapacious. The direction of that bias would **overstate**
concentration. I have no counter-source and cannot correct for it, so read the
concentration figures as the strongest version of the case.

## The estimate, and how much to trust it

Two methods, sharing no underlying data:

**A. Behavioural.** 396 firms filed a PAGA case in twelve months.

**B. Membership.** 1,400 CELA attorneys ÷ ~3.5 attorneys per plaintiff-side firm
= ~400 firms.

They land four apart. I want to be honest that this is partly luck: my divisor of
3.5 was chosen before I saw how close it would come, but it was chosen loosely,
and a divisor of 3 or 4 would have given 467 or 350 instead. The convergence is
real but it is a convergence of ranges, not of points.

**Why the range runs to 600 on the high side:**

- The 396 counts PAGA *cases filed*. Notices run above 10,000 a year. Some firms
  send a notice and settle before anything is filed in court, and they never
  appear in a filing count.
- Wage-and-hour class actions with no PAGA count attached are outside that 396
  entirely, and there were more than 5,000 California employment class actions in
  2023.
- A twelve-month window misses the firm that brings one of these every second or
  third year. Those firms still need the arithmetic when they do.

**Why the range runs to 350 on the low side:** some of the 396 filed exactly one
case opportunistically and are not really in this business. They will not buy
software for a thing they do once.

**Best estimate: ~400 firms, range 350–600.** I trust source 4 most, because it
counts conduct in a defined window rather than self-description, and because it
is the only source whose denominator is the actual activity the product serves.
CELA is second, and it is second only because its 1,400 is an attorney count that
I had to convert to firms with a divisor I invented.

## What 100 firms means

| Denominator | 100 firms is |
|---|---|
| 350 | **28.6%** |
| 396 (best single figure) | **25.3%** |
| 600 | **16.7%** |

**Achievable or delusional?** Neither word fits cleanly, so here is the honest
version. A quarter of a defined professional market is a very good outcome for a
vertical software product and a poor one for a year-one plan. Products that reach
25% penetration of a trade usually take years and usually have a salesperson. The
plan's budget is under $1,000 and its headcount is one. **100 firms is a
three-to-five-year number, not a year-one number.** State it as such and the plan
survives. State it as year one and the plan is fiction.

The more useful revision: **20 firms is 5% of the market and probably 30% of the
matters**, because of the concentration below. Twenty is a number a solo operator
can actually reach by name, one at a time, and it is a defensible first target
that 100 is not.

## Concentration, and what it does to the product

This market is not flat. It is a short, steep head and a long, thin tail.

| Tier | Firms | Share of filings | Rough filings per firm per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Head | 5 | ~24% | 400–600 |
| Upper mid | next 15 | ~26% | 100–200 |
| Tail | ~376 | ~50% | ~12 average, median likely 2–5 |

The tail average of twelve comes from dividing the residual half of ~8,850
notices across ~376 firms. The median is lower than the mean because the tail is
itself skewed. Treat both as derived, not sourced.

Three consequences, and they are the reason this note matters more than the
headline number:

**1. One price will be wrong for both ends.** A firm filing 600 matters a year and
a firm filing three cannot be sold the same seat licence. At the head, the
arithmetic is already industrialised: these are volume shops with process, a
dedicated damages person, or an outside consultancy. They are the hardest sale
and the largest contract. At the tail, a $500/month subscription is unjustifiable
against three matters a year, and a per-matter price is the only thing that
computes. Usage-based pricing, or two products.

**2. Which 100 you get changes the revenue by an order of magnitude.** One hundred
tail firms is maybe 300–1,200 matters a year between them. One hundred firms
including the top twenty is most of the state's wage-and-hour volume. The plan
should say which hundred.

**3. The head may not be a customer at all.** The five firms filing 400+ notices
a year have already solved this problem, because at that volume you cannot not
have solved it. The product's natural buyer is the tail and the upper-middle:
firms that take these cases seriously enough to need the damages model and not
often enough to have built one. That is roughly 300–400 firms, and it is a
smaller and better-defined target than "the California plaintiff employment bar."

## What I could not establish, and what would close it

- **Distinct filing firms straight from the agency.** The LWDA PAGA notice
  database is public and carries the filing firm on each notice. Counting distinct
  filers over a twelve-month window turns the 396 from `[review]` into a number
  computed from source records. That is a scraping job of maybe a day, and it
  would also produce the full filings-per-firm distribution the tail estimate
  above is guessing at.
- **CELA firm count.** Not published. The member directory presumably reveals it.
- **CLA Labor and Employment Law Section membership.** Not published.
- **Attorneys per plaintiff-side firm.** No source. My 3.5 is a judgement and it
  is the load-bearing assumption in method B.
- **Any figure at all read at primary source.** Everything here is a search
  summary. The proxy blocked all of it.
