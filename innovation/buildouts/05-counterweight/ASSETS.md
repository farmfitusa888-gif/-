# Counterweight — ready-to-send assets

Everything here is meant to be copied and used, not adapted. Placeholders are in
`[SQUARE BRACKETS]` and there are deliberately very few.

**One rule before you send anything.** Do not run any of this until the day-30
accuracy gate has passed. Every message below implies the product works. If it
does not yet, you are making the DoNotPay mistake in miniature.

---

## 1. First approach — public adjuster, 2024–25 cohort

Sent through the contact form or business email on their own website. **Not to a
scraped address.**

**Subject:** `Question about your policy review process`

> Hi [FIRST NAME],
>
> I saw you're licensed as a public adjuster in [CITY] — I pulled the TDI
> register while researching how adjusters handle policy review.
>
> Quick question, and I'm genuinely asking rather than pitching: when a denial
> letter comes in, roughly how long does it take you to read the policy against
> it and find where they don't line up?
>
> I ask because I've built a tool that does that comparison and shows you the
> two passages side by side, with a link to the page each one came from. I'm
> looking for a handful of adjusters to try it on a claim they've already
> settled — one where you already know what the answer turned out to be — so I
> can find out where it's wrong.
>
> No charge, no commitment, and I'd rather hear that it missed something than
> hear that it's great.
>
> Worth ten minutes?
>
> [YOUR NAME]
> counterweight.app

**Why it is shaped like this.** It opens with a real question rather than a
claim. It names the register, which is verifiable and slightly flattering. The
ask is to *test on a closed case*, which is low-risk and makes them the expert.
And it explicitly invites criticism, which is both disarming and genuinely what
you need in the first month.

**Do not** add a calendar link, a demo video, a testimonial, or the word "AI".

### Follow-up, 5 days later, once only

**Subject:** `Re: Question about your policy review process`

> Hi [FIRST NAME] — just closing the loop on this. If the timing's wrong, no
> problem at all.
>
> One thing that might be useful either way: I wrote up how to read a denial
> letter against a policy, including the four gaps that show up most often
> (the cited provision not saying what the letter claims it says is the
> commonest by a distance).
>
> counterweight.app/guides/reading-an-insurance-denial-letter
>
> No signup, nothing to buy. Take it if it's useful.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

**Then stop.** Two messages. A third makes you a nuisance in a community of
1,708 people who talk to each other.

## 3. The demo call — 20 minutes

**Do not present. Watch them use it.**

**0–2 min — set the frame**
> "Before I show you anything — what's the slowest part of a case for you right
> now? I want to make sure this is even relevant."

Then shut up and listen. Write down their words exactly; they are your copy.

**2–5 min — their document, not yours**
> "Do you have a closed case handy? One where you already know what the answer
> was? I'd rather use yours than a demo file — mine always works, which makes it
> useless as a test."

**5–15 min — they drive**

Let them upload. Let them click. Say nothing while it processes.

When findings appear, ask the only question that matters:
> "Is that right?"

Then:
> "What did you find on this case that it missed?"

**Write down every miss.** That list is your roadmap and it is worth more than
the sale.

**15–18 min — the arithmetic, in their numbers**
> "You said that case took you about six hours of reading. What's your hourly
> rate? …So if this saved you three of those, that's [$X]. It's $299 a month,
> unlimited cases. How does that land?"

**18–20 min — the close, or the honest no**
> "Two ways to go. You can take fourteen days and run it on live cases — no
> card, and I'll set you up today. Or if it's not there yet, tell me what would
> have to be true and I'll come back when it is."

**The second option must be genuinely on the table.** In a market of 1,708
people, a bad-fit customer who churns loudly costs more than the revenue.

## 4. Objections, with answers

**"How do I know it's not making things up?"**
> "You don't, and you shouldn't take my word for it. That's why every finding
> links to the page it came from — you're reviewing evidence, not trusting a
> machine. If a finding has no page behind it, it doesn't appear. And I'd rather
> you tested it on a case you already know the answer to than on a live one."

**"I've tried ChatGPT for this and it invented a policy provision."**
> "I believe you, and that's the exact failure this is built around. A general
> chatbot has no reason to stay inside your document. This only reports things it
> can point at a page for. It'll still be wrong sometimes — it just can't be
> wrong invisibly."

**"Does this affect my licence?"**
> "No. It never contacts a carrier, never negotiates, never signs anything, and
> never communicates with anyone. It's a tool in your practice, like your
> estimating software. The licensed activity stays entirely yours — which is
> also the reason I'm selling to you rather than competing with you."

**"$299 is a lot for software."**
> "Compared to what you bill, it's one to two hours. The question isn't whether
> $299 is a lot, it's whether it saves you more than two hours a month. If it
> doesn't, don't buy it — and please tell me, because then I've built the wrong
> thing."

**"I'll think about it."**
> "Of course. Can I ask what you'd be weighing up? If it's whether it actually
> works, take the trial — that answers it better than I can. If it's budget or
> timing, tell me when to come back and I will."

**"Can you just do the claims for me and take a percentage?"**
> "Not without a licence, and that's your licence, not mine. But that's a real
> conversation — if you ever have more claims than you can work, come back to me."

*(Note this one. It is the bridge to idea 2, and if it comes up more than twice
in the first twenty calls, that is a signal worth acting on.)*

> afterwards and yours never moves.
>
> What I want in exchange:
> - Run it on real cases and tell me when it's wrong
> - A 20-minute call after 60 days about what actually changed
> - Permission to quote your numbers, anonymously if you prefer
>
> That's it. No case study production, no logo on a website unless you offer.
>
> I'm doing this because the thing I most need right now is evidence that it
> works, and the only way to get that is from people using it on real claims.

## 8. Association outreach — after you have numbers

Send to NAPIA or the state association **only once you have real outcome data**.
Pitching an association before that burns the channel permanently.

**Subject:** `Some data your members might find useful`

> Hi [NAME],
>
> I build case software for public adjusters. Over the last 90 days [N] adjusters
> have run [N] claims through it, and the results are specific enough that I
> thought your members might want them:
>
> - Median time to review a policy against a denial: [X] hours before, [Y] after
> - Findings confirmed by the adjuster: [Z]%
> - False positives per case: [N]
>
> I'm not asking for an endorsement and I'm not asking to advertise. If it's
> useful, I'll write it up for your newsletter with the methodology attached so
> members can argue with it.
>
> Also happy to share the denial-letter guide we published, free and with no
> signup, if that's of any use to the membership.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

**Why this works and a pitch does not.** Associations exist to bring members
useful things. Give them one. The product mention is a byline, not an ad.

## 9. Words we do not use

| Never | Instead |
|---|---|
| "AI-powered" as the headline | "Reads the policy against the denial letter" |
| "Automated claim analysis" | "Finds where the documents disagree" |
| "Replaces hours of legal work" | "Cuts the reading, keeps the judgement" |
| "Guaranteed", "proven", "always" | The measured number, or nothing |
| "Our AI determined…" | "The tool flagged this passage — here's the page" |
| Anything implying a licence or a credential | — |

**This is not squeamishness. It is the FTC's DoNotPay order turned into a style
guide**, and in a profession where the buyer carries personal liability, restraint
reads as competence.
