# Overquote, the operating plan

Photograph a car repair estimate, get a plain-English read on whether you are
being overcharged, before you say yes. Written 2026-08-31.

Budget assumption throughout: **under $1,000**. Solo operator. No paid ads.

**Read section 2 before section 3.** This is the seventh of seven and the plan
below is written to disprove it cheaply, not to launch it.

---

## 1. The one-sentence version

**77% of drivers distrust repair shops and cannot check a single line of the
estimate in front of them. But the consumer arithmetic needs 1,035 paid checks
a month with no marketing budget, the core function is now a free thing a phone
already does, and the only route that reaches $30k is a single bundling deal a
solo operator with no track record is not yet able to close.**

---

## 2. Why this one is seventh of seven

**The pain is real, well-measured, and stable. The business is not.**

| Figure | |
|---|---|
| US drivers who distrust auto repair shops | **77%** [AAA consumer survey] |
| Parts markup, independent shops | **25–60%** |
| Parts markup, dealerships | **40–80%** |
| Repair cost increase, early 2022 → June 2024 | **~25%** |
| Drivers delaying repairs over cost | **82%** |

The AAA number is the one that should stop you, and not in the way it first
reads: **"that number hasn't meaningfully moved in years."** A statistic that has
not moved through a decade of RepairPal, Openbay, certified-shop networks and
warranty badges is not an untapped opportunity. **It is evidence that several
funded companies have already attacked this and the number did not move.**

And a press release found during research uses the exact same statistic as its
hook, *"AAA: 77% of Drivers Fear Mechanic Overcharges. One Service Fixes That."*
**Someone is already selling into this precise sentence.**

### Four structural problems, in the order they kill it

**1. The money does not work at consumer prices.**

| Model | To reach $30k/mo | What that requires |
|---|---|---|
| $29 per check | **1,035 checks/month** | At [ASSUMPTION] 2% visitor-to-paid conversion, ~**52,000 monthly visitors** — a large SEO property, in a category where incumbents hold a decade of domain authority |
| $9/month subscription | **3,334 subscribers** | At [ASSUMPTION] 10% monthly churn on a product used twice a year, **333 net-new subscribers every month forever** just to stand still |

Both numbers are marketing-spend numbers, and there is no marketing spend.

**2. The core function became free.** Anyone can photograph an estimate, hand it
to a general-purpose assistant on their phone, and get a plain-English read in
thirty seconds for nothing. **That is the actual competitor now, not RepairPal.**
It did not exist when the incumbents launched and it removes the reason to pay
$29 for a translation.

What survives that: licensed labour-time data, local price comparison, and a
published accuracy record. **Two of those cost money and the third takes years.**

**3. Being wrong can put someone in an unsafe car.** Telling a driver that a brake
or steering repair is unnecessary is a different class of error from telling them
a cabin filter change is padding. The product must be conservative by design,
which shrinks what it is allowed to say, which shrinks what anyone will pay for it.

**4. The one route that reaches the target is a single enterprise deal.** Insurers,
roadside clubs and warranty administrators are correct as buyers. At [ASSUMPTION]
$0.25 per member per month, **$30k/month is one club with 120,000 members.** That
is not a consumer app plan, it is an enterprise BD plan, a 12–24 month sale, by
a solo operator with no track record, into organisations that buy on procurement
cycles.

### What this plan therefore is

**Not a launch plan. A disproof plan that costs under $600 and takes 60 days.**

That is genuinely the best thing about Overquote: **it is the cheapest business in
the portfolio to kill.** Fifty real estimates and one paid mechanic's afternoon
settle the central question, and a hundred real users with a real price wall
settle the second. **If you are going to spend anything on this, spend it finding
out fast that you should not continue.**

## 3. The customer, with real numbers

### The consumer, who is well-measured and cannot carry the business

**82% of drivers are delaying repairs over cost.** That is the market, and it is
also the problem: **the person most motivated to check an estimate is the person
least willing to spend $29 to check it.** A driver deferring a $900 repair because
money is tight is not a reliable buyer of a $29 opinion about it.

**Willingness to pay is [NOT ESTABLISHED] and is the single most important unknown
in this plan.** What would settle it: put a real price wall in front of 100 real
people with real estimates and count the cards. That is days 31–60 below.

### The buyers who could actually pay

| Segment | Why they pay | Reachable solo? | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Roadside clubs and auto clubs** | Member benefit, differentiator, and AAA itself published the distrust statistic | **No, not yet.** National procurement | [NOT ESTABLISHED] |
| **Credit unions** | Auto lending plus member education; small enough to talk to one person | **Yes — the best first B2B target in this plan** | [NOT ESTABLISHED] — NCUA publishes a free, complete credit union list |
| **Insurers** | Member benefit; claims-adjacent | No | [NOT ESTABLISHED] |
| **Extended warranty / vehicle service contract administrators** | Their loss ratio rises when shops overcharge | Maybe | [NOT ESTABLISHED] |
| **Small commercial fleets, 10–50 vehicles** | They pay repair bills every week and have no in-house estimator | **Yes** | [NOT ESTABLISHED] |

**Two honest notes on that table.**

**The warranty administrators may not have the problem.** They already employ
adjusters who authorise repairs and negotiate labour times with shops. Overquote
would be competing with an internal function, not filling a gap. **Test that
assumption in one conversation before building anything for them.**

**Small fleets are the most interesting untested segment and they are a
hypothesis, not a finding.** A 30-vehicle fleet paying, say, $199/month for
estimate review has real money in it and needs only ~150 customers rather than
1,035 transactions. **[NOT ESTABLISHED] whether any fleet operator wants this or
already has it covered by a relationship with one shop.** What would settle it:
ten conversations with local fleet operators, landscaping, plumbing, courier,
non-emergency medical transport, which costs nothing but a morning.

**Credit unions are the recommended first B2B target** because they are the only
one on the list where a solo operator can reach the decision-maker. NCUA publishes
the complete list of federally insured credit unions free. A community credit
union with 40,000 members already sends a member newsletter and already runs
financial-education content. **A member-benefit pilot there is a real conversation
a solo operator can actually have.**

## 4. Positioning

**Overquote reads the estimate you were just handed and tells you, in plain
words, which lines you can check and which questions to ask.**

Against what the driver would otherwise do:

| Alternative | Why they leave it — or do not |
|---|---|
| **A free general assistant on their phone** | **The real competitor, and it is free.** Overquote wins only on things a chatbot cannot do: published flat-rate labour hours, real local part pricing, and an accuracy record. **If the product cannot do at least one of those, it has no reason to be paid for and should not be built** |
| **Ask a friend who knows cars** | Free, trusted, available to most people, and genuinely good. This is honestly the strongest alternative and it is rarely acknowledged |
| **RepairPal / Openbay and the estimator sites** | A decade old, real traffic, real brand. They give a price range for a job, before you have an estimate. Overquote reads the specific document you are holding, in the waiting room |
| **Certified shop networks and warranty badges** | The industry's answer: trust a different institution. **Overquote gives the customer their own competence instead** — a translator, not a referee |
| **Say yes and hope** | The default, and 77% of them do it while distrusting the person they are saying yes to |

**The line that does the work:** *three specific questions to ask, so the shop can
see you are being advised.* In a market built on asymmetry, visibly removing the
asymmetry is what changes the price, and it is deliverable without ever telling
anyone a repair is unnecessary.

**What we never say:** that a repair is unnecessary. That we are mechanics. That
the shop is cheating you. That we guarantee a saving. **The product's own name
accuses somebody, and the copy must therefore be more careful than the name.**

## 5. Pricing, and the honest arithmetic

| Model | Price | To reach $30k/mo | Verdict |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Per check | $29 | 1,035/month | **Needs traffic that needs spend that does not exist** |
| Subscription | $9/mo | 3,334 subscribers | **Worse — churn on a twice-a-year product eats it** |
| Member benefit, PMPM | $0.25 [ASSUMPTION] | 120,000 members | **Works, and requires one deal** |
| Fleet | $199/mo [ASSUMPTION] | ~150 fleets | **Untested, and the only route with real money per customer that a solo operator can sell** |

### Why the subscription is worse than it looks

A subscription only works if the product is used often enough to be remembered at
renewal. **A car needs an estimate reviewed perhaps twice a year.** A $9/month
subscriber pays $108/year for two uses and cancels the month they notice. At
[ASSUMPTION] 10% monthly churn, steady state is adds ÷ churn, so **holding 3,334
subscribers requires 333 new ones every month, permanently.**

The transaction price is at least honest about what the product is.

### The per-customer cost that may end it

**Labour time guides. Mitchell, ALLDATA, Motor, are commercial licensed data.**
Pricing is not public. **[NOT ESTABLISHED]**, and it is the single most important
number to obtain, because if a per-lookup or per-seat licence costs a meaningful
fraction of $29, **the product as designed cannot exist at consumer prices.**

What settles it: request a quote from all three. It costs an email and a week.

**The design consequence, decided in advance.** There is a version of this product
that needs no licensed data at all:

- **Parts prices are checkable free.** Retail listings and dealer parts sites give
  a real number for the same part number. This is the most checkable form of
  overcharging and the one nobody checks
- **Bundling logic needs no licence.** Whether the quoted work is implied by the
  stated symptom is a reasoning question, not a data lookup
- **"Shop supplies," disposal fees, and percentage add-ons** are visible on the
  document itself
- **Labour hours** are the part that needs the licence, **so v1 says nothing
  about labour hours and says so openly**

**That is not a compromise, it is a better v1.** It is cheaper, it is safer, and
it is honest about its own limits, which is the whole posture of this portfolio.

## 6. Go to market, $0, and specific

**Every channel below is organic. None of them, individually or together, produces
1,035 paid checks a month, and the plan does not pretend otherwise.** They are
here because they are how you get the 100 real users the day 31–60 test needs.

### Channel 1, Answer estimate questions in public, without linking

`r/MechanicAdvice`, `r/AskACarGuy`, model-specific owner forums, and Facebook
owner groups have people posting photographs of estimates and asking exactly this
question, every day, for free.

**Answer them properly. Do not link anything.** A genuinely good public read of a
stranger's estimate builds the only asset that matters here, which is a reputation
for being right. It also produces the graded corpus for §7 at zero cost.

### Channel 2, "Read this estimate with me" video

The single highest-organic-reach format for this content, on YouTube and TikTok,
at zero cost. Blur the shop name. Walk the lines. Say what is checkable and what
is not. **This is the one channel where the format itself demonstrates the
product**, which no amount of landing-page copy does.

### Channel 3, Search intent that already exists

The brief's own observation is the keyword list: **people literally type "was I
overcharged for car repair."**

- "was I overcharged for car repair" · "is this repair estimate fair"
- "[part name] replacement cost [vehicle]" · "what are shop supplies fees"
- State Attorney General and BBB auto-repair complaint pages rank for this
  intent and indicate where the volume is

**Low competition on the long tail, brutal competition on the head terms**, which
RepairPal has held for a decade.

### Channel 4. Credit unions (the only real B2B channel a solo operator can work)

NCUA publishes the complete free list. Filter to community credit unions with a
member newsletter and an auto-loan book. **Offer a free member-benefit pilot in
exchange for a case study and permission to quote the numbers.** One pilot is
worth more than every consumer channel above combined, because it is the only one
that points at the model in §5 that actually reaches the target.

### Channel 5, Local fleet operators

Ten conversations, one morning, zero cost. Landscaping, plumbing, courier,
non-emergency medical transport. **This tests the most interesting hypothesis in
the plan for the price of a morning.**

### What we are NOT doing

- **No paid ads.** No budget, and consumer acquisition at $29 a transaction will
  not survive a CPC auction against companies with funding.
- **No shop partnerships.** Shops as a distribution channel directly contradicts
  a product whose entire promise is that it is on the customer's side. **Pick a
  side. The customer's.**
- **No app store launch.** A mobile app is a build cost and a distribution
  problem, both before the central question is answered. Web, and a photo upload.
- **No content mill.** The corpus of publicly-answered real estimates is worth
  more than thirty generic articles.

## 7. The 90-day plan, written to kill it cheaply

### Days 1–14. Ask the two questions that can end it

| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | Know the labour-data cost and whether the safety-constrained product still has something to say |
| **Do** | Request licence quotes from Mitchell, ALLDATA and Motor. In parallel, take 10 real estimates and mark which lines a **no-labour-data** product could still meaningfully check |
| **Spend** | $0 |
| **Gate** | **If fewer than half the lines on a typical estimate are checkable without licensed data, and the licence costs more than ~$5 per check, stop here.** That is a two-week, zero-dollar kill and it is a good outcome |

### Days 15–40, Fifty estimates, graded by a real mechanic

| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | The two numbers that decide the product |
| **Do** | Collect **50 real repair estimates** from public forums and consumer complaint threads. Run the model. Pay an **ASE-certified mechanic** to independently grade the same 50 |
| **Measure** | (a) **correct-flag rate** — how often it flags a genuinely inflated line; (b) **false-flag rate** — how often it flags a legitimate one |
| **Spend** | **$300–500. The best use of the budget in this plan** |
| **Gate** | **False-flag rate above 10% → stop.** A tool that cries wolf at an honest shop is worse than nothing, and it is the failure mode that ends the business |

**The false-flag number is the one that matters, not the correct-flag number.**
Missing an inflated line leaves the customer where they already were. Falsely
accusing an honest shop damages a business, and eventually damages you.

### Days 41–60. Put a real price in front of 100 real people

| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | Answer the willingness-to-pay question, which is [NOT ESTABLISHED] and load-bearing |
| **Do** | Work channels 1–3. Deliver the free read to 100 real people with real estimates. Then put a **real $29 price wall** in front of the next cohort — a live checkout, not a survey. Surveys about intent to pay are worthless here |
| **Ship** | A conversion number |
| **Gate** | **Fewer than 5 of 100 pay $29 → the consumer route is dead.** Written down now, so it cannot be renegotiated in month three |

### Days 61–90. Test the only route that reaches the target

| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | One credit union pilot conversation and ten fleet conversations |
| **Do** | Work channels 4 and 5, carrying the accuracy numbers from days 15–40 as the entire pitch |
| **Gate** | **Zero pilot interest from 10 credit unions and 10 fleets → there is no B2B2C route available to you at your current level of credibility, and with the consumer route already failed, the business is over** |

### What month 12 honestly looks like

**Almost certainly not $30,000/month, and the plan should not pretend a path
exists that the arithmetic does not support.**

The realistic good outcome is: the accuracy numbers hold, the consumer route
produces a few hundred dollars a month, one credit union runs a member-benefit
pilot, and **twelve months in you have a credible case study and a product that
is worth something to someone who has distribution.** That is a real outcome. **It
is not a $30k/month business in year one and calling it one would be the exact
overselling this portfolio was built to avoid.**

## 8. Metrics

| Metric | Target | Why it is the one that matters |
|---|---|---|
| **False-flag rate on legitimate lines** | **<10%, target <5%** | The failure mode that ends the business. Tracked from check one and published |
| **Correct-flag rate on inflated lines** | **>60%** [ASSUMPTION target] | If it cannot beat a careful reading, there is no product |
| **Safety-critical false negatives** | **Zero, permanently** | Any instance where the product implied a needed brake, steering, tyre or fuel-system repair was skippable. **This metric has no acceptable non-zero value** |
| Free read → paid conversion | >5% | Below this the consumer model is dead. See §11 |
| Repeat use within 12 months | >20% | Determines whether subscription is even arguable |
| Licensed labour-data cost per check | <$5 | Determines whether the full product is affordable at all |

**The first three are tracked from customer one and published.** DoNotPay was not
fined for having a bad product. It was fined for **never testing whether its
service worked** and advertising anyway, and Overquote, which tells people
whether a professional is cheating them, has more reason to hold that line than
anything else in the portfolio.

## 9. Legal posture

**What Overquote is:** an information tool that reads a document the customer
already possesses and explains it.

**What it is not, in the terms and on the site:** a mechanic, a diagnostic
service, a safety inspection, a guarantee of price or quality, or an accusation
against any named business.

| Exposure | Handling |
|---|---|
| **Personal injury from unsafe deferral** | **The one that ends the company.** Hard product rule: brakes, steering, suspension, tyres, airbags, fuel and cooling systems are **never** described as unnecessary. For those lines the product checks **price only** and says so explicitly on the output |
| **Defamation / trade libel** | Never name a shop. Never say "this shop is overcharging you." The output describes **lines**, not businesses, and phrases everything as a question the customer can ask |
| **FTC deceptive claims** | No savings guarantees. No "one service fixes that." Accuracy claims backed by the published false-flag rate or not made at all |
| **Licensed data terms** | Flat-rate labour guides carry redistribution restrictions. **Displaying licensed hours to consumers may breach the licence even when you hold one — confirm before designing the output around it** |
| **Automotive repair consumer statutes** | Several states regulate estimates, authorisations and repair disclosures. Overquote does not perform repairs and does not authorise them, but **confirm with counsel that reading and commenting on an estimate for a fee triggers nothing in the launch state** |

**The human in the loop, where the law does not require one but the risk does.**
An **ASE-certified mechanic on a paid retainer reviewing a rolling sample of
outputs**, from the first customer, is the licensed-human rule from the thesis
applied to a category where nobody is technically required to be licensed.
**Budget for it as a permanent line, not a launch expense.** It is also the only
credible answer to "why should I pay you when a free chatbot reads it too."

## 10. Risks, ranked by what actually kills it

**1. The consumer will not pay, and there is no budget to find 1,035 who will.**
The central risk, and the plan tests it by day 60 for under $600. *Mitigation:
none available. It is a fact to discover, not a problem to manage.*

**2. The core function is free on every phone.** A general assistant reads an
estimate in plain English for nothing. *Mitigation: the only defensible ground is
licensed labour data, real local part pricing, and a published accuracy record —
and if none of those is achievable within budget, there is no product.*

**3. Licensed labour data may be unaffordable or unusable in consumer output.**
*Mitigation: the no-labour-data v1 in §5, decided in advance rather than
discovered late.*

**4. A false flag at an honest shop.** Reputational, and in the aggregate legal.
*Mitigation: the <10% gate before any launch, the questions-not-accusations output
format, and never naming a business.*

**5. A safety-critical false negative.** Lower probability, catastrophic. *Mitigation:
the hard category rule in §9, which is architectural rather than a prompt
instruction.*

**6. The B2B2C route requires credibility you do not yet have.** One deal replaces
all the marketing, and that one deal is a 12–24 month enterprise sale. *Mitigation:
credit unions and small fleets, which are the versions of that buyer a solo
operator can actually reach.*

**7. A decade of incumbents own the search terms.** RepairPal, Openbay and the
press release already using the 77% figure. *Mitigation: long-tail intent and the
"read this estimate with me" format, neither of which is a real defence against a
funded competitor.*

**8. Shops will fight it**, and shops are the distribution channel for several
adjacent products. *Mitigation: accepted. Pick the customer's side and stay there.*

## 11. Kill criteria

Written now, before the tests, because this is the business most likely to be
kept alive by enthusiasm past the point where the numbers said stop.

| By | If | Then |
|---|---|---|
| Day 14 | Labour data costs **more than ~$5 per check** **and** fewer than half of a typical estimate's lines are checkable without it | **Stop.** Two weeks, zero dollars, correct answer |
| Day 40 | **False-flag rate above 10%** | **Stop.** Do not launch it. A tool that cries wolf at an honest shop is worse than nothing |
| Day 40 | **Correct-flag rate below 40%** | **Stop.** It is not beating a careful human reading and there is nothing to sell |
| Day 40 | **Any safety-critical false negative in the 50** | **Stop and re-architect.** Not tune — re-architect. Then re-run the full 50 |
| Day 60 | **Fewer than 5 of 100 pay $29** at a live checkout | **The consumer business is dead.** Do not soften the price and retry. Move to day 61–90 or stop entirely |
| Day 90 | **Zero pilot interest from 10 credit unions and 10 fleets** | **Kill it.** Consumer failed, B2B2C is unreachable, and there is no third route |
| Month 6 | **Under $2,000/month** from any route | Kill it. This is the seventh-ranked idea; six better uses of the same hours exist in this portfolio |

**The rule that makes these real: write the number down before the test, and do
not renegotiate it afterwards.**

## The honest summary

**Do not start here. The portfolio README already says so, and building the plan
out in detail confirms it rather than rescuing it.**

The pain is genuine and enormous, 77% distrust, 82% delaying repairs, markups of
25–80%. And it has been genuinely enormous for a decade while funded companies
attacked it and the number did not move. **The information asymmetry is real; the
willingness to pay $29 to close it is not established, and the arithmetic needs
1,035 of those payments every month with no way to buy attention.**

**What is worth doing:** the 60-day, sub-$600 disproof in §7. It is the cheapest
decisive test in the portfolio. Collect the fifty estimates, pay the mechanic,
publish the two numbers, and put a real price wall in front of a hundred real
people.

**If the false-flag rate holds and people pay, that is genuine new information and
this plan becomes worth rewriting.** If they do not, you will have learned it in
two months for the price of one afternoon of a mechanic's time, which is a much
better outcome than spending a year finding out.

**And if the numbers do come back good, the right move still is not the consumer
app.** It is to take the published accuracy record to a credit union, a roadside
club, or a warranty administrator, and let someone with distribution pay for it.
