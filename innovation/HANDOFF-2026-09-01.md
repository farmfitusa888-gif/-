## 1. Where this ended up, in one paragraph

The project began as seven business ideas and narrowed, through research, to
one: **Backpay**, wage-and-hour damages software for California. It is the only
idea in the set that ranked first on both revenue potential and liability, which
is the only place in the whole portfolio where those two did not trade against
each other. **Countercite**, insurance denial analysis, is second and complete
enough to sit on a shelf. The other five are documented and not recommended.

The free half of Backpay is built and works. The paid half is not started.

## 3. The decisions that would be expensive to reconstruct

**Backpay over the other six**, on a liability screen that had never been run.
The load-bearing finding was not "sell to professionals" but something sharper:
what protects a business like this is **how the fee is computed**, flat and
outcome-independent, rather than who signs the cheque. A contingent fee would
convert software into regulated adjusting in every launch state at once.

**Professional-only, and the consumer line closed.** Two agents reached this
independently, one on legal grounds and one on arithmetic. A denied claim is an
event rather than a condition, so a policyholder base churns near 50% a month;
holding 100 paying consumers would need 50 new ones every month forever. The
professional base churns near 3.5% and needs four.

**The market is ~400 firms, not the unknown the plan assumed.** 396 filed at
least one PAGA case in the year to May 2026. It is steeply concentrated: five
firms take a quarter of filings, twenty take half. The top five are probably not
customers, because at 400-plus notices a year they have already built this.
**First target revised from 100 firms to 20.**

**The payback is proven.** One damages model costs a firm about $2,300 in cash
and carries about $16,000 of lodestar value. A $299 subscription pays for itself
at **one matter every 7.5 months**, or one per quarter on a deliberately
pessimistic reading.

**Inbound only, which is slower than the plan said.** Realistic: $8-12k a month
at month 12, $25-35k at month 24. Not 6 to 12 months. Kill criteria are written
down in `research/10-INBOUND-ONLY-GTM.md` before the tests rather than after.

**Nothing is sold that is not built.** `platform/check-features.mjs` fails the
build if the site claims to be selling while any feature is still planned, if a
legal document still has a blank, or if copy appears that sells direct to a
consumer. Each of those guards was verified by breaking it deliberately and
confirming the build refused.

## 4. The entity question, stated fairly

This is what stopped the work, so it gets a straight section rather than a
footnote.

**Nothing is required.** A sole proprietorship is the default legal form in the
United States. You file nothing and pay nothing to be one.

**The advice is generic.** "Form an LLC before you take money" is said to
freelance designers, Etsy sellers and consultants alike. It is not a signal
about this business. Reading it as one is the single most likely way to
misjudge what is here.

**Backpay needs it less than most.** The buyer is a law firm rather than a
distressed consumer, the realistic claim is contractual, and a
limitation-of-liability clause between two businesses generally holds. That
clause is drafted, in `legal/TERMS-OF-SERVICE.md`.

**A single-member LLC creates no new tax.** By default the IRS treats it as a
disregarded entity: the same Schedule C, the same total tax, no separate
business return. Detail in `legal/01-ENTITY-AND-TAX.md`.

**The free half never needs one.** The calculator and the stub checker take no
money from anyone and can be published permanently as an individual.

The four worries recorded on 1 September were: personal exposure, the $300 and
the paperwork, tax complexity, and that needing an entity signals a risky idea.
The fourth is the one worth revisiting, because it is the only one that is
factually wrong rather than a preference. The other three are legitimate and
nobody should talk anyone out of them.

## 5. If it resumes, the order

1. **Buy one domain.** `backpay.co` if the plan continues, about $12. Free
   static hosting exists but needs an address. Nothing else in phase one costs
   anything.
2. **Ship the free tools.** Already built. Cloudflare Pages, $0.
3. **Build the query set.** The pages someone finds after a short paycheck,
   generated programmatically, each carrying one unique artifact so it clears
   the thin-content gate. This was the next task and is not started.
4. **Only then** the application, the legal blanks, and the entity, in that
   order, and none of it before someone is about to pay.

## 6. If it does not resume

Nothing is wasted in a way that matters. The engines are general, the site
generator drives any number of sites, and the research stands on its own. Total
spend to date: **nothing.** No domain, no entity, no subscription, no
infrastructure.

That was deliberate, and it is why stopping here costs nothing.

## 7. Open items, honestly

`node tools/status.mjs` counts these live. At the time of writing: 160 passages
still marked `[NOT ESTABLISHED]` across the research and plans, and 68 blanks in
the legal drafts. Most of the 160 are deliberate refusals to guess, and many sit
in documents for businesses now dropped. The 68 blanks are things only the owner
can fill: entity name, address, effective date.

The largest genuine gap is not on either list. **There is no application.** No
accounts, no upload, no billing, no hosting. Everything built is either a static
page or a script. That is the bulk of the work between here and revenue, and no
part of this handoff pretends otherwise.
