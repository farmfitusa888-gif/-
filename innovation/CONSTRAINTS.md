# The constraint set

Written 1 September 2026, after the owner ruled out a business that needs an
entity. These are hard, not preferences. Anything proposed from here is checked
against this list first, and a proposal that fails one of them is not a proposal.

Recorded because they arrived one at a time across a long conversation, and a
list held in someone's head gets quietly relaxed.

## The seven

1. **No direct customer relationship.** Nobody pays the owner for a product or
   a service. Revenue arrives from advertising, affiliates, or a platform that
   is the merchant of record and sells to the end user in its own name.

2. **No entity, ever.** Sole proprietor. Any model that requires or strongly
   advises incorporation fails. This is the constraint that produced the other
   six, so it is the one to test a proposal against first.

3. **No outreach.** No cold email, no calls, no direct messages, no forum
   posting to find users, no sales conversations of any kind. Replying to
   someone who arrives on their own is fine. Hunting is not.

4. **It runs itself.** In the owner's words: "This project should run itself
   for the most part." Anything needing daily human attention fails.

5. **Under $1,000 to start**, and no ongoing spend that must be paid before
   revenue exists.

6. **Solo, built with AI.** One person, no hires, no contractors.

7. **No regulated subject matter** where being wrong causes someone a
   quantifiable loss they would want back. Insurance, medicine, law and
   employment all failed on this. It is why the last idea was dropped.

## The income target, honestly

The stated goal has been $20,000 to $50,000 a month. Under constraints 1 and 3
together that is a much harder combination than it was, because removing the
customer removes the highest revenue per user and removing outreach removes the
fastest channel.

That target is being tested rather than assumed. If the evidence says a
no-customer structure cannot reach it for a solo operator in any reasonable
time, the number moves and the owner decides against a real figure instead of
a hopeful one.

## What this does not rule out

Worth stating, because the list above reads restrictive and the space is larger
than it looks. None of these are excluded:

- Publishing anything, on any subject, supported by advertising
- Affiliate revenue, with the disclosure rules the FTC actually requires
- Selling assets through a marketplace that handles every transaction
- Free tools that answer one urgent question and carry advertising
- Turning public data nobody can use into something people can
- Licensing work to a business, which is a contract rather than a customer base
