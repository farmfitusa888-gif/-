# 5 — Arm the advocates

**Every idea in this project competes with a human charging 10–50%. Sell to them instead.**

`PROVEN — the buyer is already identified and already paid` · Buyer: the advocate · Form: software

---

## The gap

This project's research kept turning up the same character: a solo professional
who fights institutions for individuals, charges a percentage or a high hourly
rate, and is capacity-limited by how many hours they personally have.

| Advocate | What they charge | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Public adjusters | **10–20%** of settlement (10–15% typical residential) | [review] |
| Medical bill negotiators | **10–35%** of savings | [review] |
| Property tax appeal firms | **25–50%** of first-year savings | [review] |
| IEP / special education advocates | **$75–300/hour**; **10–15 hours** per family, **$1,500–2,250** typical | [review] |
| Probate attorneys | **$200–500/hour**; **$3,000–7,000** flat for simple estates | [review] |

Look at what they have in common:

1. **Demand exceeds their capacity.** The IEP research states it directly: *"the
   system is stacked against low and middle-income families"* and cost is a
   substantial barrier [review]. That is unmet demand at the current price.
2. **They are paid on outcome**, so anything that increases throughput or win rate
   is worth real money to them, immediately and obviously.
3. **They are drowning in exactly the work AI is now good at** — reading policies,
   assembling records, drafting letters, tracking deadlines.
4. **They buy their own tools.** Solo practitioners with a business credit card.

## What it is

Not one product — **one engine, sold into whichever of these niches responds
first.** They differ in vocabulary and document type, not in shape: ingest a pile
of institutional documents, find the contradictions, produce the argument, track
the clock.

For an IEP advocate specifically, as the sharpest example:

- Read the school's evaluation, the current IEP and the parent's records together
- Surface **where the IEP fails to address findings in the school's own
  evaluation** — the core of every winning argument, and pure cross-referencing
- Draft the parent's letter, in the register that gets a response
- Track the statutory clocks, which are strict and which parents routinely miss

## The innovation

The others in this project try to *replace* the expensive human. This one
concedes that the human is legally necessary and often genuinely better, and
**sells them the leverage to serve five families in the time they now serve one.**
It is the least glamorous idea here and quite possibly the best business: no
consumer marketing, no regulatory exposure, a buyer who converts your product
into money the same week, and — because these advocates already take contingency
— an obvious upgrade path into taking cases yourself.

**It is also the compliant vehicle for ideas 2 and 4.** Where a licence is
required to negotiate a claim, this is how you participate legally.

## Money

| Price | To reach $30k/mo |
|---|---|
| $149/mo | **202 advocates — 17 net adds a month** |
| $199/mo | **151 advocates — 13 a month** |
| $299/mo | **101 advocates — 9 a month** |

An advocate billing $150–300/hour recovers $299/mo in **one to two billable
hours**. That is the easiest payback argument in this document by a distance.

**One hundred customers is the whole business.** That is reachable without a
marketing budget — these are small, well-networked professional communities with
conferences, associations and referral habits.

## Risks

- **Small, fragmented populations.** I could **not establish headcounts** for
  public adjusters, IEP advocates or patient advocates. Several of these
  professions may number in the low thousands, and 100–200 customers out of 3,000
  is a very different sales problem than out of 300,000. **This is the largest
  open question and it is answerable from association membership rolls and state
  licence registries — public data, not phone calls.**
- **Selling software to solo practitioners is famously hard**: low budgets, high
  churn, low technical tolerance.
- Each niche needs its own vocabulary and document handling, so "one engine" is
  partly aspiration.
- If it works, it arms the competitors of ideas 2 and 4.

## Where to start — no phone calls required

**Pull the public licence registry.** Public adjusters are state-licensed and
those registries are public records; IEP advocate certification bodies publish
directories. Count them. That single afternoon of desk research converts the
biggest unknown here into a number.

Then build for whichever population is largest, and measure the only thing the
buyer cares about: **hours saved per case, verified against their own timesheets.**
